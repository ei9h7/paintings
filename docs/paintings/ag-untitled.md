## Overview
A portrait of AG, an individual who chooses to remain unidentified beyond their initials. This work explores figuration through unconventional materials and surfaces, challenging traditional portraiture approaches.

## Technical Details

**Medium:** Tempera, acrylic, and water color crayon  
**Surface:** Black felt primed with gesso  
**Dimensions:** [See commit for documentation]

## Artistic Analysis

### Surface & Ground
The use of black felt as a ground is significant—it subverts the traditional white canvas and immediately establishes a darker, more intimate visual space. The gesso primer creates a prepared surface that's both structured and textured, allowing the pigments to adhere while maintaining the fabric's tactile quality.

### Medium Integration
The combination of tempera, acrylic, and water color crayon creates interesting contrasts:
- **Tempera & Acrylic:** Traditional fast-drying media providing structure and opacity
- **Water Color Crayon:** Adds unpredictability and softer, more vulnerable marks
- The interplay between wet and dry media creates visual tension

### Subject & Identity
The deliberate anonymity of the subject adds conceptual depth. By remaining unidentified, AG becomes both specific (a real individual) and universal (any person). This creates a paradox in portraiture—the work is intimately personal while maintaining a protective distance.

### Figuration
The portrait approach grounds this work in representation, distinguishing it from the more abstract works in the collection like *Lichen* and *Trapped*. Yet the unconventional materials and surface suggest a resistance to traditional portraiture conventions.

## Conceptual Notes

- Exploration of identity and anonymity
- Challenge to traditional portrait painting
- Integration of "non-art" materials into fine art context
- Intimate personal documentation without exposure
