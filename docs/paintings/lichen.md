## Overview
An exploration of organic texture and form created through layered processes on black felt. The work features a recurring humanoid character that emerges organically throughout ei9h7's body of work—possibly a subconscious visual motif. The title references the appearance of lichen (the symbiotic organism), capturing a feeling rather than literal representation.

## Technical Details

**Medium:** Tempera on black felt  
**Techniques:** Poured, combed, and scratched  
**Surface:** Black felt (prepared ground)  
**Dimensions:** [See commit for documentation]

## Process & Technique

### Layered Mark-Making
The combination of pouring, combing, and scratching creates a dynamic, multi-directional surface:
- **Poured:** Tempera flows across the black felt, creating organic pools and gradients
- **Combed:** Linear marks carved through wet pigment, introducing rhythm and direction
- **Scratched:** Incised marks that remove paint to reveal the black ground, creating high contrast and definition

This process-driven approach prioritizes material engagement over predetermined composition, allowing the work to develop through the artist's physical interaction with the materials.

### The Humanoid Figure
The central humanoid character that reappears across multiple works suggests a recurring visual vocabulary—whether intentional or subconscious. This motif connects *Lichen* to works like *The Head* and appears in *Unfinished Arguments*, suggesting an ongoing artistic investigation into human form and presence.

### From Process to Naming
The title emerges *after* completion—a retrospective naming that captures what the work evokes rather than what it depicts. The organic, textured surface with embedded humanoid form reminded the artist of lichen's complex, interwoven structure. This approach to titling reflects a conceptual practice where observation and intuition guide interpretation.

## Conceptual Analysis

### Abstraction & Recognition
While primarily abstract, the work maintains a figural anchor (the humanoid character), creating tension between pure abstraction and representational suggestion. Viewers may or may not perceive the figure, allowing multiple valid readings of the same work.

### Organic vs. Controlled
The pouring and combing techniques introduce controlled spontaneity—the artist guides materials through systematic processes, yet remains responsive to emergent results. This mirrors natural growth processes, connecting technique to the lichen metaphor.

### Surface as Subject
The black felt ground is not passive backdrop but active participant. The contrast between black and tempera colors creates luminosity through opposition, making the surface itself a primary concern of the work.

## Connection to Collection Themes

**Recurring Motifs:** The humanoid figure links to *The Head*, *Unfinished Arguments*, and *AG (untitled)*  
**Process-Driven:** Similar to *Assorted Marbles*, emphasizing technique and material over representation  
**Figuration/Abstraction Dialogue:** Bridges representational and abstract work within the collection
